# Multi-query training objective: audit and plan (2026-10-13)

Question: does the multi-query objective make sense? That is, Hungarian loss plus scheduled sampling, where teacher-forced steps feed the golds in shuffled order. Two concerns were raised:

1. The gold order is shuffled, so the model sees a different order every time.
2. When golds are fed in, the model can copy them (identity function) and the Hungarian loss becomes trivially low.

Context: the QAMPARI weekly summary for Oct 5–11 (`reports/weekly_summary_2026-10-12.md`, artifact "QAMPARI Multi-Query Week"). Single-query (53.30 test) beats every multi-query run. The best are `masked` at 47.65 (five near-identical queries, q–q cos 0.94) and `hps 0.5` at 46.14 (one strong query plus weak extras). Multi-query runs lose dev score late in training, and under full or saturated sampling, output 0 alone becomes useless.

Status (2026-10-22): **diagnostics D1–D5 and experiments E1/E2/E3/E5 are done on both sides.** Joint-side results and conclusions: section "Session J summary" below. Frozen side: F's Status-log entries and its fusion reports. Short version: `hungarian_seq` (E1) is the one consistent gain (joint best 65.2 / 48.40); fed_norm, detach and input/output layers tie; the late collapse needs a trainable doc encoder and is avoided by dev selection. *(Original status, 2026-10-13: plan only, nothing implemented yet.)*

## How the objective works (code)

`training/inf_retriever/src/inbatch.py`, `EmbeddingModelDocEncNoProj.forward` (~line 746):

- Output 0 is the question's last-token state.
- At step t, the model is fed either the shuffled gold π(t) (detached) or its own output t, chosen per example. The probability of feeding its own output is `sampling_rate`.
- The model's own output is **not detached** (`predicted = next_hidden`), so gradients flow back through it.
- Output t+1 is the next last-token state.
- The loss matches all k outputs to all k golds in one Hungarian assignment (`HungarianContrastiveLoss`, or the masked / hps / div variants).
- Schedule (`finetuning_multi.py:839`): `sampling_rate = min(1, step / (sampling_saturate_frac * total_steps))`. The default ramp runs linearly from 0 to 1 over 2500 steps. Selected checkpoints (750–1500) were trained at rates of 0.3–0.6, which is mostly teacher forcing.

## Analysis

- **The copy shortcut is real.** Because matching is global, output t+1 can be matched to the gold π(t) that was just fed in. Copying solves k−1 of k slots, and output 0 gets the leftover random gold. Copying may be almost free: the fed vector is a final-layer hidden state, probably with a much larger norm than a token embedding, so it would dominate the residual stream at that position. This last part is a hypothesis and is untested (D2).
- **The shuffle is fine on its own.** A random order suits a set target. The flaw is that the target for step t+1 is "any gold" rather than "a gold not yet fed".
- **Full sampling at the end of the ramp (the user's point) is only a partial fix.**
  1. Selected checkpoints come from the middle of the ramp, where copying still pays.
  2. When the model feeds its own outputs, copying becomes repeating the previous query. Plain Hungarian penalises that, because the question's other golds are negatives. The masked loss does not, which fits `masked` ending with five copies of one query.
  3. The teacher-forced phase teaches copying rather than "find what's still missing", so nothing carries over to the free-running phase.

## Findings from the training logs

Source: the wandb history of the 16 clean_v3 QAMPARI runs, exported to `reports/train_log_csv/<run>.csv` by `extract_wandb.py` in this folder. Three agents analysed it. Their plots, tables and scripts are in `copy_shortcut/`, `late_collapse/` and `loss_terms/`.

**The logs support copying.**
- **Later positions look easier when gold is fed.** In `multi` at low rates, the matched cosine is flat at about 0.68 for positions 1–7, against about 0.53 for output 0. In `fullsamp` (no gold ever fed), it falls with position (0.67 → 0.47).
- **The extra fit tracks teacher forcing.** On positions 1–4, the train–eval cosine gap minus the same gap on output 0 is about 0.23 × (1 − rate), with r = 0.89 over 12 runs × 10 evals.
- **Teacher forcing shows up as repeated queries.** At step 250, eval `repeat_pct` orders by how much teacher forcing each run had seen: fullsamp 46, sat25 64, sat50 74, multi 82, masked 92. The untrained model is at 69.
- **Plain Hungarian unlearns the repeats; masked does not.** `multi` falls to 32 by step 2500. `masked` stays at 88–94, and the div penalty only brings it to about 70.
- **There is a mid-ramp transition cost.** Plain Hungarian training loss rises with the rate (multi 0.57 → 0.78 near a rate of 0.55) and only recovers near 1.
- **One prediction failed.** Masked training accuracy does not fall as the rate rises, because collapsed queries satisfy the masked loss either way.

**The late collapse is a separate problem, and its cause is open.**
- **Onset follows the sampling rate, not the step count.** It starts when the rate reaches about 0.8–1. For sat50 that is step 1250. The damage grows with time spent at a rate of 1 (sat25 > sat50 > multi).
- **Training metrics improve while eval does not.** After the switch, training loss collapses (sat25 0.97 → 0.035) and train align_margin reaches about 0.3. Train gold–gold cos falls from 0.56 to 0.43, while eval does not improve. This looks like overfitting by pushing a question's golds apart in the jointly trained document space.
- **The agents disagree on the gradient explanation.** One reads `C_multi` (frozen document encoder: in-training dev MRR down 2.1, `C_single` flat) as evidence for the feedback-gradient explanation. The counter-argument is that `C_multi`'s frozen golds are already 0.85 alike, so its rising loss (0.96 → 2.3) may just mean the task is too hard there. D3 and D4 address this.
- **The hps terms do not fight.** Their step-to-step changes correlate at 0.97–0.99. The div penalty is active (never zero) but does not fix shared top-1 (still 18–19%).

**Metric caveats (the first two were checked in the code):**
- `eval_acc` / `eval_mrr` in `finetuning_multi.py` evaluate() (~528–552) score **only output 0** for multi runs, and `best_model` is chosen on that. The metric is also in-batch and saturated: 87–96%, even before training. Weekly-report selection uses dev retrieval, so the reported numbers are unaffected.
- `eval_step_j_acc` divides by all dev queries (`multi_step_total`, ~601/635), but step j only exists for queries with more than j golds. The fall-off after j≈4 is an artifact. Corrected, accuracy is flat at 85–95% for j ≤ 7. Values for j ≥ 8 come from 11 queries or fewer.
- `train/step_j_teacher_cos_sim` is cos(output j, the gold fed *after* output j), which is effectively a random sibling gold, not the matched target.
- Plain Hungarian and hps runs log no training accuracy. `train/pairwise_cos_sim` is identical to `train/query_pair_cos`. `stdq`/`stdk` are flat.

## Diagnostic results (2026-10-13)

Probe: `probe/tables.md` (28 checkpoints; D2 160 dev queries, D3 96, so each value is approximate, but the patterns hold across checkpoints). Log analysis: `d5/`. Code: `tools/objective_probe.py` on branch `objective-fixes`.

**Copying under teacher forcing is the default, from initialization.**
- In the untrained model, with gold teacher-forced, output t+1's top-1 is the gold just fed in **94%** of the time.
- In every ramp-trained checkpoint it is still **88–96%**, for every loss (multi, masked, hps, hps_w025 incl. step 2500, C_multi, A_multi).
- It fades only after a long stretch at sampling rate 1: fullsamp 62–73%, sat50@2500 44%, sat25@2500 18%. Only then does "top-1 = a gold not yet fed" rise (33–49%). The teacher-forced phase teaches almost none of "find what is still missing"; that skill appears late, while the model is already overfitting (D5).

**Copying turns into repetition when the model feeds its own outputs.**
- Masked: consecutive queries share their top-1 gold 90–93% of the time (cos 0.96–0.98), which explains its five near-identical queries.
- Plain multi: 13–22%. Same-question golds as negatives break up the repeats, but nothing trains a later query to find a *different* gold, so its queries are diverse but individually weak.
- Frozen C/A_multi: 69–83%, because frozen golds are too close (median cos 0.85) for the negatives to push repeats apart.

**Mechanism: input scale (measured: norms and behaviour; inferred: the residual-stream account).**
- A position's state in the Qwen2 pre-norm transformer is a residual stream: `h_0` = the input vector; each layer adds an update computed from a normalized copy of the state, so update size is set by the weights, not by the input; the pooled embedding is `final_norm(h_L)`.
- An ordinary token has `|h_0|` ≈ 0.62, and the layers build the state almost from scratch. A fed-in gold is a final-layer output with norm ≈ 195 (base/frozen; 50–140 after joint training): **80–300× a token embedding, an input scale the model never saw in pretraining.**
- The layers' usual-size updates sit on top of that large vector, so the final state keeps pointing mostly along it. The final norm rescales but keeps direction, so output t+1 ≈ the fed gold: copying with no learning required.
- **Evidence that size is the cause:** with `--fed_norm token` (same direction, rescaled to token size) the copy advantage disappears at initialization. Teacher-forced cos to the fed gold falls to 0.47 against 0.51 for the best unfed gold; without rescaling it is 0.62 against 0.52 (smoke, 1-GPU).
- **Not yet measured:** how large the layer updates are, and how much of `h_L` lies along `h_0`. A per-layer cos(`h_l`, fed vector) probe, with and without fed_norm, would confirm it.
- **Second consequence, a train–inference mismatch:** teacher-forced inputs are golds (norm up to 195), while self-fed inputs are the model's own outputs (≈ 65 in the frozen runs, 40–140 joint). The model trains on inputs of one size and runs on another.

**Output 0 is pulled around by the later steps (D3).**
- At rate 1, the gradient reaching output 0 through the fed-back input is as large as its own gradient or larger: batch-level 1–3× for most checkpoints (masked 4–39×).
- The two are nearly orthogonal (cos ≈ 0, ~50% of examples opposing): later steps push output 0 in directions unrelated to its own retrieval job.
- Late in the sat runs, output 0's own training loss is ≈ 0, so nearly all its updates come from later steps (per-example ratio 150–280). That fits output 0 drifting until it is useless alone (test k = 1 < 1). `hps` anchors it with the single-query term.

**The late collapse is overfitting after the switch to self-feeding (D5).**
- Ramp runs: the train–eval align_margin gap shrinks as the rate rises (~0.2 → 0.06). That is teacher-forcing inflation.
- Runs at rate 1: the gap bottoms out at the switch, then grows steadily to 0.20–0.24 by step 2500.
- The old in-training eval (output 0 only) couldn't see this. Open: document-space vs query-side overfitting; the frozen sat50 run (F) answers it.

**Possible fixes for the input-scale problem** (beyond `--fed_norm token`, which is in flight: J `j_hps_fednorm`, F `C_fednorm`): see the next section.

### Fixes for the input-scale problem: options

| Option | What it changes | Fixes copying? | Fixes train/inference scale gap? | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `--fed_norm token` (implemented) | rescale every fed vector to the question's mean token-embedding norm | yes at init (smoke) | yes | no parameters; direction kept, so a model could still learn to amplify it |
| Learned input adapter: RMSNorm (gain initialised to token scale) + linear / small MLP on the fed vector | maps a fed vector into the input-embedding space instead of reusing a final-layer vector as a token | yes if its output is token-scale; can re-learn copying if copying stays rewarded, so pair with E1 | yes | the original `src/model.py` stack had an `input_projection`; the inf_retriever classes are "NoProj" |
| Output head: MLP after the pooled state, query = head(h); feed back h (pre-head) | separates "state carried to the next step" from "query that is scored" | **no on its own**: copying lives at the input; a head can learn ≈ identity | no | does address D3: output 0's fed-back state is no longer the vector scored for retrieval. Teacher forcing would need an input adapter (golds are in query space, h is not) |
| E1 `hungarian_seq[_used]` (implemented) | copying is no longer rewarded (fed golds are forbidden targets) | removes the incentive, not the mechanism | no | in flight (J, F) |
| No feedback: k learned query-slot tokens after the question, generated in parallel | removes the autoregressive input altogether | yes (nothing is fed) | yes (no exposure bias) | a different model; useful as a baseline for whether autoregression helps at all |

### Conclusions

1. **E1 (`hungarian_seq`, forbid matching an output to an already-fed gold) is the only change that helps consistently.** On plain Hungarian it is a large gain (+2.8 dev / +8.9 test). On hps 0.5 it is smaller (+2.2 / +2.3, ≈ 1 SE), because the single-query term already does part of the job. Both seq runs reach ≈ 64–65 dev / 47–48 test, the best joint multi-query results, still ≈ 5 test points below joint single-query (53.30). Recommended default multi-query loss: `hungarian_seq`.
2. **E5 (`--fed_norm token`) removes the copy shortcut but not the gap.** It cuts teacher-forced copying (copy rate 0.44 vs ≈ 1.0), yet ties its baseline on dev in both families. The input-scale mechanism is real but not what limits retrieval. The learned input adapter was therefore not built for the joint side (user rule, 2026-10-13). F's frozen input/output-layer runs (`C_*_ioproj`, `inproj`, `outproj`) all tied as well.
3. **E2 (`--detach_fed_output`) does not help, alone or on top of E1.** It ties on dev; on test it is +0.2 on hps and +4.3 on plain Hungarian, below seq. So the D3 feedback gradient into output 0, though large (1–3× its own), is not the bottleneck. Detach also does not stop the late decline.
4. **The late collapse (every run worse at 2500 than at its peak) has two parts.**
   - With a trainable doc encoder it is large (−8 to −13 dev); F's frozen-doc runs decline only ≈ 1 point (D4(a)), and the train–eval align_margin gap grows to 0.20–0.24 only in joint runs (D5).
   - E3 (stop doc gradients at 1250) removes most of it under hps_seq: test holds at 48.59 at 2500 (vs 41.81), dev falls 3.6 instead of 8. It gives no better dev-selected checkpoint.
   - E3 does not rescue plain Hungarian with an early switch (`sat50_docstop` 60.6 / 39.92 at the dev-selected step 750, s1000 60.2 / 43.31, → 49.2 / 31.64 at 2500), so there the query side alone also overfits after the switch to rate 1.
   - In practice dev selection already avoids the collapse; the selected steps are 750–1250.
5. **Copying (D2) is the default from initialization** (top-1 = fed gold 94% in the base model, 88–96% in ramp-trained checkpoints) and turns into repeated queries under the masked loss. See "Diagnostic results" above.

6. **Learned input/output layers help a little on the joint model** (`j_multi_seq_ioproj`, user request 2026-10-22), though not on frozen (F). At dev-selected step 750 the score is 66.4 / 49.53, the best joint multi-query result: +2.4 dev / +2.3 test over multi_seq, about one paired SE. Large / small-distance test: 39.88 / 58.89 (multi_seq 36.90 / 53.33; single 41.67 / 61.67). Query 1 alone gives 48.02 test. Query pair cosine is 0.64 (multi_seq 0.63), so the gain is not from more diverse queries. The run still declines by 2500 (51.0 / 34.84). The gap to single is now 3.8 test.

## Plan

### 1. Diagnostics (high → low priority)

**D1: Fix and extend training logging.** Small code change, no GPU. This is a prerequisite: without it, the E-runs' curves cannot be interpreted.
- Fix the `eval_step_j_acc` denominator.
- Add a multi-output dev metric: the share of a question's golds reached by its outputs' top-1s, with free-running generation. Select `best_model` on it instead of output-0 MRR.
- Log the copy rate, split into teacher-forced and free steps: cos(output t+1, fed gold t), and how often Hungarian matches output t+1 to fed gold t.
- Log training accuracy for plain Hungarian and hps.
- Rename `teacher_cos_sim`.

**D2: Direct copy probe on existing checkpoints.** One `srun`, about 1 GPU-hour.
- Checkpoints: base, and `multi`, `masked` and `hps` at steps 750, 1500 and 2500.
- Teacher-force dev batches and measure:
  - cos(output t+1, fed gold t) against the golds not yet fed;
  - how often the match goes to the fed gold;
  - whether output t+1's top-1 is the fed gold.
- Also compare the fed-vector norm with the token-embedding norm.
- Shows whether copying is already there at initialization. Gates E5.

**D3: Where output 0's gradient comes from.** One `srun` on existing checkpoints; no training run.
- Output 0 (q0) has two jobs:
  1. It is a query, scored by its own loss term L0.
  2. When sampled, it is fed back as an input. Since it is not detached, the losses on outputs 1…k−1 also backpropagate into it.
- Split its gradient into two parts:
  - g_own = ∂L0/∂q0, which pushes q0 toward being a good query;
  - g_feed = ∂(L1+…+L_{k−1})/∂q0 through the input path, which pushes q0 toward being a convenient input.
- Hypothesis: as the rate goes to 1, g_feed dominates or opposes g_own. That would explain why output 0 alone becomes useless under full or saturated sampling, and why hps (which strengthens g_own) resists it.
- Method: use per-term losses and `torch.autograd.grad` with respect to q0 on training batches at rates 0, 0.5 and 1. Run on the multi, sat25, fullsamp and hps checkpoints. Log ‖g_feed‖/‖g_own‖ and cos(g_feed, g_own).
- Reading the outcome:
  - **Small or aligned g_feed:** the feedback path is not the cause. Deprioritise E2 and favour the D4 overfitting explanation.
  - **Large, opposing g_feed that grows with the rate:** the feedback path is the cause, and E2 (detach) should fix it.

**D4: Why performance falls after the rate reaches 1.** Two short training runs. There are two candidate explanations:
- (i) **The switch itself:** a model that learned on gold inputs must now work from its own outputs. D3's mechanism is one version of this.
- (ii) **The document-space shortcut:** the jointly trained document encoder lowers the Hungarian loss by pushing a question's golds apart, which hurts retrieval.

Two experiments separate them:
- **(a) sat25 with a frozen document encoder** (the `C_multi` setup), against plain sat25. A frozen encoder cannot push golds apart, which rules out (ii). The existing `C_multi` uses the linear ramp and only reaches a rate of 1 at the very end, so it does not answer this. Compare each run's drop relative to its own pre-switch peak.
  - **Frozen run degrades about as much:** the cause is (i). Follow up with D3 and E2.
  - **Frozen run degrades much less:** the cause is (ii). Follow up with E3.
- **(b) sat50 with evals every 50 steps over steps 1000–1600**, around its switch at step 1250. Use D1's metric, and plot training loss, gold–gold cos, align_margin, output-0 quality and dev on one axis.
  - **Golds drift apart first, then dev drops:** supports (ii). If the drift leads by 100 steps or more, it is an early-stop signal for E3.
  - **Output 0 degrades while the document space is stable:** supports (i).
  - Caveat: sat50's `step-1000/checkpoint.pth` is 7.1 GB, probably weights only, without optimizer state (unverified). Resuming from it would reset Adam's moment estimates near the switch. The clean version reruns from step 0.

**D5: Free checks on the existing logs.**
- Train-minus-eval align_margin over steps.
- Corrected per-position accuracy split by gold count.
- hps: cos(output 0, output 1) over training. Position 1 is weak at w = 0.5: eval align 0.51 against about 0.65 at output 0. This checks whether the single term crowds position 1.

Suggested order: D1 and D5 now. D2 and D3 as `srun` jobs (D3 is cheaper than D4, so run it first). D4 only if D3 is inconclusive or to confirm.

### 2. Improvement experiments (high → low priority)

All on the clean_v3 QAMPARI setup with the hps 0.5 recipe and D1 logging. Headline metric: MRecall@100 at k = 5 with RRF; checkpoints chosen by full dev.

**E1: Step-by-step matching that excludes used golds.**
- Match outputs in generation order. Step t may only match golds not already used by an earlier step: a fed gold on teacher-forced steps, or the earlier output's assigned gold on sampled steps.
- Used golds stay in the softmax denominator as negatives.
- This blocks copying when gold is fed and repetition when the model feeds its own outputs, so the "find what's still missing" signal is the same at every rate.
- In the teacher-forcing-only case it reduces to masking the cost matrix: −inf for (output t, gold π(s)) with s < t. The diagonal stays feasible.

**E2: Detach the fed-back output** (`next_hidden.detach()`), as a 2×2 with E1. This is the cheapest way to separate the explanations for the late collapse. Its priority depends on D3.

**E3: Limit the document-space shortcut after the switch.** Options:
- early stopping on D1's metric;
- a lower document-encoder LR, or L2-SP, once the rate reaches 1;
- capping the length of the free-running phase.

Its priority depends on D4.

**E4: Teacher forcing in the model's own order.**
- First generate the k outputs freely with no gradient, and match them to the golds.
- Then teacher-force with the matched golds in that order; step t's target is its matched gold.
- This removes shuffle noise, copying and most of the gap between training and inference.
- Only worth doing if E1 helps but leaves a gap.

**E5: Rescale the fed vector to token-embedding norm.** Only if D2 shows copying at initialization.

Possible follow-up: revisit the sampling schedule (linear ramp, saturating, or capped below 1) once E1 and E2 are in.
